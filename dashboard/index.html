Every protocol still waiting on its token, ranked by what the launch would be worth.

The board starts from the forty protocols with at least $50M of TVL that record no token, then adds every smaller launch candidate the snapshot holds. Each one is valued from its own figures — its TVL, its category’s fitted multiple, and a size factor — against the 88 launched protocols that pass the same comparability rules. Seven-day growth and age are shown for every row but carry no weight: the fitted evidence says they do not predict the multiple (see Validation). Implied value is this entry’s own claim, never a price, and never a prediction of whether or when a token is issued.

Honest language: a protocol “records no token” when the snapshot records none for it. That is not proof it has none.

How the value is worked out

Implied value = TVL × shrunk category multiple × size factor. The category multiple is the shrunk log-mean of market-cap / TVL over the cleaned comparable peers in that category — log-ratios clipped to [−4, +2], shrunk toward the global log-mean with k = 5 — and the size factor is exp(slope × (log TVL − TVL centre)). Both are fitted at runtime from the sealed snapshot’s own launched protocols, not set by hand. The same function scores every row; no protocol name is ever special-cased.

The fitted model beats v1 out of sample (log-RMSE 2.106 vs 2.386; paired MSE difference +1.260 ± 0.428, t = +2.94) and beats the naive global median (2.277). Model F is statistically identical (paired MSE −0.008 ± 0.081, t = −0.10); G is adopted because winsorizing caps the thin-category fragility — the highest effective multiple on the board is 0.922×, where the unwinsorized fit reaches 1.633× on a single-peer category.

Every row carries an 80% interval of ×0.075–×13.3 around its implied value, from the out-of-sample residual SD of 2.016 log units on comparable protocols. The interval is wide because the multiple is genuinely hard to predict: log(market-cap / TVL) spans 14 log-units across launched protocols, and the features jointly explain little (in-sample R² 0.186). The interval is honesty about that, not precision.

Why growth and age carry no weight

Seven-day growth and tenure are shown for every row, but both multipliers are 1.0000. The fitted evidence: the growth slope is −3.82, but it falls to −0.31 when the five highest-ratio protocols are dropped — the two highest ratios (rain 400×, nexo 69×) both have a 7-day TVL change of about −37%, a denominator artefact of a collapsing TVL, and the association points the wrong way (fast growth would be rewarded). Age has no measurable effect: its correlation with the log-ratio is +0.002, and its fitted slope is ≈ 0 once the five highest ratios are dropped (−0.25 → −0.00). Keeping the old hand multipliers would assert what the data contradicts.

Thin categories: Risk Curators — ranks #1–3, #6, #8–9, #11, #13 — rests on exactly one comparable peer (api3, 2.975×), and 89 of 178 rows sit in categories with two or fewer comparable peers. Shrinkage (k = 5) and winsorizing bound the damage, but the peer count next to every multiple above is the honest view of it.
